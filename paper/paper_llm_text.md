@@TEXT_LLM_GEN
The two smaller models wrote faulty programs for 11 of 22 (qwen3.5-4b) and 19 of 31 (clover-35b-a3b) unique compiled candidates. Of these 30 faults, 29 called sum_values and therefore ignored units, validity flags, or both; the remaining one skipped validation. Each passed its discovery example because that example contained only valid records in liters. All 30 came from the terse and example-only requests; with the full specification and documented tools, every compiled candidate of every model was valid. DeepSeek wrote no faulty candidate. With signature-only documentation, many responses reached the 4,096-token output budget before producing a program (16 for qwen3.5-4b, 11 for clover-35b-a3b, 10 for DeepSeek). Without a library, a task that receives a fresh sample would succeed 23.3 % of the time with either smaller model and 58.3 % of the time with DeepSeek.

@@TEXT_LLM_ADMIT
First-success admission accepted every faulty candidate, and evaluation success fell to 40.0–63.3 % for qwen3.5-4b and 46.7–63.3 % for clover-35b-a3b across seeds. Replay, with or without composition checks, admitted no faulty candidate and rejected no valid one for any model. Both LLM verifiers also admitted no faulty candidate, so the models detect this fault class when asked to check for it, but both rejected valid candidates: self-verification rejected 9 of 11, 8 of 12, and 2 of 9 valid candidates for the three models, and evidence verification rejected 3 of 11, 6 of 12, and 0 of 9, counting unparseable verdicts as rejections. Over-rejection reduced library size but not success in this benchmark, because at least one valid skill remained in every library; in a task family with a single valid program per task kind, the same rejection rates would leave tasks without a skill. Replay used 615 to 1,335 tool calls per run and no model tokens; the LLM verifiers used 14,170 to 80,402 completion tokens per model across all candidates, in addition to their tool calls.
